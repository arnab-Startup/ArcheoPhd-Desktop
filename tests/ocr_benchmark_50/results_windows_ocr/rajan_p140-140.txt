The third part is dedicated to detailed catalogue of artifacts. The final fourth part of the report carries the results of the analysis carried out by the specialists like geologists and environmentalists and also critical comments and opinion expressed by the experts like epigraphists, numismatists, historians and other scientists. The extent of each section of the report depends on the nature of the site and the material. It is very difficult to provide any fixed framework. However, an attempt is made below to give an outline of the report. This outline may be altered to suit the character of the site and nature of the research but generally, all the excavation report carry the general format specified below. 1. Preliminaries The preliminary part of the report contains the title page, preface, acknowledgements, contents, list of tables and illustration. The title page should carry the name of the site and important feature of the site. The preface generally carries brief information on the works that are being carried out in the excavation. The acknowledgement carries the various funding agencies that extended the financial support; the various individuals, Departments and Scientific Institutions that involved in the excavation. Table of the contents carries the sequential headings or chapterisation of the report. The list of tables and illustrations covers the total numbers of tables, line drawings, maps and photographs. If the photographs or drawings are received from different organisation then it has to be suitably acknowledged. 2. Introduction The complete locational aspect of the site like taluk, district, nearest town, local name of the site, traditional name, means of access, latitude and longitude, mean sea level and any other geographical information may be given. So that any feature archaeologist who wish to re-visit the site can locate the site easily. The condition of the site at the time of discovery, during and after excavation may be given. This information is necessary as latitude and longitude as most of the sites are completely removed after the excavation work due to construction works like Publication 261 dams and industries. The environmental description of the site like geology, soil context, present climatic condition, flora and fauna, etc should be given in the introduction part itself. 3. Preliminary research All the previous exploratory or excavation work carried out by individuals or institutions must be chronologically placed in this chapter. The information such as the historical background of the site obtained through literary, epigraphical, numismatics and foreign accounts, the date of discovery, etc. must be recorded in this chapter. 4. Excavation Each excavation is being carried out to resolve certain problems. To resolve this problem certain hypotheses would have to be proposed and there would be a clear cut programmes to test these hypotheses in the field. Therefore, the aim, objective, scope and hypothesis formulated in due course would be given in this chapter. The size of the site or mound or burial would be given along with the extent of the work. The methodology followed in the excavation, number and type of specialists involved in the work and type of work and methodology carried out by the specialists may be drawn in this chapter. 5. Stratigraphy The complete stratigraphical description of the site must be give with illustration. The purpose of the level by level description is to faithfully reconstruct the site with each of its features. This stratigraphical data serves as a base for any future reinterpretation of the site or material. Aerial dimensions; horizontal and vertical dimensions of each cultural level; stratification; soil character; character of each cultural phases and any datable evidence in relation to stratigraphy should be given in this chapter. 6.
