The Three Age System The Danish scholar C.J.Thomsen (1788-1865) was invited in 1816 by the Danish Royal Commission for the Preservation and Collection of Antiquities to catalogue the findings for exhibition at Copenhagen University Museum. Thomsen was aware of the Lucretius' Three-Age scheme and he also knew that Biblical and Classical texts were suggesting that bronze had been used before iron. By keeping this in mind, he started classifying the prehistoric remains of the Museum. He was aware •that certain stone tools continued in Bronze Age and certain bronze tools in Iron Age. He, therefore, never attempted to sort the material mechanically; rather he analysed the material based on the style, decoration, shape and context. This helped him to differentiate the material within the specific period. Though his arrangements were opened to the public as early as 1819, his guide book Ledetraad til Nordisk Oldkyndighed (Guide Book to Scandinavian Antiquity) appeared only in 1839 which later appeared in English in 1848 with the title A Guide to Northern Antiquities. Based on material, he classified them into a Stone Age, a Bronze Age, and an Iron Age. Scholars found the usefulness of the system throughout Europe and later it became popular.
