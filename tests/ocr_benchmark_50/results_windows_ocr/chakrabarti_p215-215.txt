He noted that the cores found in the river bank areas were singularly well-forrned and differed from the cores found in the hills. I am much disposed to believe that the cores found in the Indus were made by a different people from those who chipped their flakes on the hills around.59 The difference suggested is, in fact, between the cores of the Indus civiliza- tion and the earlier cores found in the Rohri hills. W.T. Blanford's acumen in this regard will be very clear from his observations on the Suktagendor material collected by Major Mockler. The Harappan identity ofSutkagendor was established by Stein but as early as 1877 Blanford was recording at Sutkagendorwhat must be considered Harappan material now. Amongst the specimens on the table from Sutkagendor, 40 miles north- west ofGwadar, are some very well shaped flint knives, precisely such as we might expect to have been split off from such cores as those from Sakhar on the Indus, which I exhibited in 1875 .. .. These knives were found together with several articles of pottery ... one of the best of which is a vessel resembling a drinking cup (pl. II, fig. l) Other articles found at Sutkagendor comprise cubes like dice cut in stone, stone and pottery beads, spheres of burnt clay resembling marbles and a few fragments of copper ornaments. The "vessel representing a drinking cup" is an Irrius goblet and the illustration in Blanford's article is the first illustration of the type.
