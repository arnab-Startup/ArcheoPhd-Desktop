Metal objects should not be covered with plaster because the salts in the plaster can migrate to metal, bringing about a faster rate of decay. Generally, coins are received special attention as they give a date to the site. Bronze If the bronze artifact is impounded with incrustation, immerse the object in a 30% solution of formic acid and allow it to soak until solution become coloured. The object also wrapped up in an aluminium foil and immersed. Watch the action and remove it immediately if the acid appears to be strong for the metal. Wash the object with several baths of distilled water and dry it. Once the object like coin is cleaned, give it a surface coating of a neutral wax and P VA. Then remove the object, bathe and rinse in distilled water and allow it to dry. Iron Iron objects are subject to rusting that carries deep into the metal. A thin core of the iron encased in ferric oxide and ferric hydroxide remains. Archaeologist often finds difficulty in preserving the very shape of the material. In the field, soak the specimen in an acrylic solution (like B 72) because the general preservative PVA is slightly acidic to metal. Copper, silver and gold Copper alloy, silver and gold objects are to be removed as soon as they are exposed and that they be protected from the ambient environment. The process of decay is slowed or halted while these objects were buried but resumed on their exposure to the air. It can be treated with PVA and gauze bandage applied over the specimens. It should be placed in low humidity atmosphere. Data sheet Data sheet has to be maintained for the all the objects and treatment of each object has to be recorded. While sending the object to the laboratory, the archaeologist should furnish all the details regarding its find-spot, physical, biological and chemical characteristics of the microenvironment and the problem encountered at the time of excavation. This would be most useful for the conservator for further treatment. Natural Preservation of artifacts in extreme condition Dry preservation The shortage of water in extreme aridity or dryness ensures the prevention of destructive microorganism to flourish. The fine example of this sort of preservation is seen in Egypt where the pre-dynastic period human remains have survived intact with skin, hair and nail without any mummification or coffins.
