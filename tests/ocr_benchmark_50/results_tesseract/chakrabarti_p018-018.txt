The Englishman J. Fryer visited Kanheri and Elephanta in 1672:

We steered by the south side of the bay to touch at Elephanta, so called
from a monstrous elephant cut out of the main rock, bearing a young one on
its back . . .. All along, the walls are loaded with large giants, some with
eight hands making their vanquished knight stoop for mercy . .. this seems
to be of later date than that of Canorein.*

The Revd. J. Ovington saw them in 1689. In Elephanta there are “the
representations of the first race of mortals, which, according to the account of
their chronicles, were all giants.””*3 Ovington’s explanation of the origin of the
term “‘pagode” is of considerable interest:

A pagode is the heathen temple or a place dedicated to the worship of their
false gods, and it borrows its name from the Persian word pout which
signifies idol: hence pout Ghuda, a temple of false gods, and from thence
pagode®

Still later, Captain A. Hamilton reported on Elephanta and ‘“‘asked the
inhabitants of the island, who were all Gentoos or Gentiles, about twenty in
number, if they had any account, history or tradition who made the caves . ..

but they could give no account.”*
