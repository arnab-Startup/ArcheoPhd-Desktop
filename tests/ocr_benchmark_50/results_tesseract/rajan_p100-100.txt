Iron

Iron objects are subject to rusting that carries deep into the metal,
A thin core of the iron encased in ferric oxide and ferric
hydroxide remains.  Archaeologist often finds difficulty in
preserving the very shape of the material. In the field. soak the
specimen in an acrylic solution (like B72) because the general
preservative PVA is slightly acidic to metal.

Copper, silver and gold

Copper alloy, silver and gold objects are to be removed as soon as
they are exposed and that they be protected from the ambient
environment. The process of decay is slowed or halted while these
objects were buried but resumed on their exposure to the air. It
can be treated with PVA and gauze bandage applied over the
specimens. It should be placed in low humidity atmosphere.

Data sheet

Data sheet has to be maintained for the all the objects and
treatment of each object has to be recorded. While sending the
object to the laboratory, the archaeologist should furnish all the
details regarding its find-spot, physical, biological and chemical
characteristics of the microenvironment and the problem
encountered at the time of excavation. This would be most useful
for the conservator for further treatment.
