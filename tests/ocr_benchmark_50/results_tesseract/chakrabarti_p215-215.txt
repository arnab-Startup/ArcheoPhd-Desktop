A major observation in the context of the ““flint cores and flakes from Sakhar
and Rohri” in Sindh was made by Blanford in 1875. He noted that the cores
were found both in the Indus channel and on the hills around Rohri. He noted
that the cores found in the river bank areas were singularly well-formed and
differed from the cores found in the hills.

I am much disposed to believe that the cores found in the Indus were
made by a different people from those who chipped their flakes on the hills
around.®

The difference suggested is, in fact, between the cores of the Indus civiliza-
tion and the earlier cores found in the Rohri hills. W.T. Blanford’s acumen in
this regard will be very clear from his observations on the Suktagendor
material collected by Major Mockler. The Harappan identity of Sutkagendor
was established by Stein but as early as 1877 Blanford was recording at
Sutkagendor what must be considered Harappan material now.

Amongst the specimens on the table from Sutkagendor, 40 miles north-
west of Gwadar, are some very well shaped flint knives, precisely such as we
might expect to have been split off from such cores as those from Sakhar on
the Indus, which I exhibited in 1875 . . .. These knives were found together
with several articles of pottery ... one of the best of which is a vessel
resembling a drinking cup (pl. II, fig. 1).... Other articles found at
Sutkagendor comprise cubes like dice cut in stone, stone and pottery beads,
spheres of burnt clay resembling marbles and a few fragments of copper
ornaments.%

The “‘vessel representing a drinking cup” is an Indus goblet and the
illustration in Blanford’s article is the first illustration of the type.

1927. This was very much a general treatise, based to some extent on his
fieldwork in the Ghatsila area of Singhbhum, but in the context of the period, it
