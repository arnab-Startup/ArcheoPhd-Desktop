Though Darwin vehemently opposed this mistreatment, his human

12" century. French Calvinist Librarian Isaac de La Peyrere raised
it as a major issue in 1655. He argued that the biblical Adam was
the ancestor of the Jews alone, while ancestors of other human
groups had been created separately. In 1774, Edward Long argued
that Europeans and Negroes were separate species. Morton

Among the scholars who placed the racism with
archaeological input was John Lubbock (1834-1913), the Lord of
Avebury in England. His two books Pre-historic Times, as
Hlustrated by Ancient Remains, and the Manners and Customs of
Modern Savages (1865) and the second book The Origin of
Cuvilisation and the Primitive Condition of Man (1870) went
through several editions. He advocated that as a result of natural
selection, human groups had become different from each other not
only culturally but also in their biological capacities to utilise
culture. He believed that technologically less advanced people are
culturally and intellectually more primitive than civilised ones.
This emphasises the alleged superiority of the Europeans over
other human groups. This increased the confidence of the
Europeans particularly British. They felt that it is their right to rule

the technologically and culturally less developed people or one

It also helps to give better justification for their rapid colonisation

in America, Africa and Australia, It influenced the interpretation
of archaeological data in many parts of the world. By promoting a
sense of ethnic identity, it played a significant role in the
unification of Germany in 1871 and afterwards by expressing the
pride of the Germans. This approach was challenged in the late
19™ century by growing nationalism and declining faith in the
benefits of technological progress. A concern with historical and
ethnic problems led the archaeologists to pay increasing attention
to the geographical distribution of distinctive types of artefacts
and artefact assemblages. This led to the formulation of theories
like culture-historical archaeology, processual, post-processual
and behavioural archaeology in the 19" century, which are
discussed in chapter four.
