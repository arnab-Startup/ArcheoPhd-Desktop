History of Archaeology

History of Archaeology starts with the man’s curiosity of knowing
the past. In most of the societies, man’s curiosity of understanding
the past has been satisfied with myths and legends. In certain
societies there are some reliable written records and visible
monuments providing a chronological framework and information
about what had happened in the past. Sometimes, these could not
give complete picture of the past. Human interest in unraveling or
understanding their past gave a good thrust for locating, collecting
and interpreting artefacts of their ancestors. This curiosity laid a
foundation for archaeology. Man’s quest for understanding the
past had a long history. In this long history, archaeology passed
through different stages of development. These developments,
both in methods and techniques, have been traced in this chapter.

The history of archaeology is generally understood as the
history of great discoveries like the tomb of Tutankhamun in
Egypt, the lost Maya cities of Mexico, prehistoric human remains
of Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania and the great city of Harappa and
Mshenjodaro of Indus valley civilization. These monuments and
artefacts were used to forge the identity of the nation. The ancient
places like Jerusalem, Kusinagar, river Saraswathi, Ayodhya,
Dwaraka, Poompuhar and Pataliputra serve as a source of
!information to project nation’s glorious past. Such interests have a
- strong religious, political and social component. However, they
play an indirect role in preserving the great monuments and
antiquities. For instance, a- collection of ancient Babylonian
artefacts including inscriptions amassed by Bel-Shalti-Nannar, a
daughter of King Nabonidus, in the 6" century BC has been
considered as the first known museum of antiquities. Si-ma Qien,
the first great Chinese historian of 2 century BC, visited ancient

ruins and documented those things in his work Shi Ji. Such

systematic study of the past was valued and used for national
integration by stressing the common cultural heritage. Islamic,
Christian, Buddhist and Hindu traditions are being used to show
